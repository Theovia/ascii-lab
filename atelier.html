Head-tilt parallax · 60 fps

An atlas is a way of seeing.

We render classical paintings through a fragment shader that samples each pixel as luminance and color, picks a character from a 42-glyph ramp, and draws it back in the painting's own palette.

Each cell is four pixels wide. The cursor doesn't deform the image — it tilts it. As you move your hand across the canvas, the entire painting rotates a few degrees and shifts a few pixels toward you, the way a face slowly turns to follow a sound at the edge of a room.

The atlas is not exhaustive. It is selective. We render the works that withstand simplification — Vermeer, Caravaggio, La Tour, Kobra. Light becomes shape; shape becomes character; character becomes plate.
